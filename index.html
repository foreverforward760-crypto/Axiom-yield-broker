Ghost-Carrier Detection Filter
Fraudulent MC pattern screening — absence of operational history is the signal
How ghost carrier detection works

Legitimate carriers build operational history through natural growth cycles — periods of high volume followed by capacity contraction, route changes, and recovery. This creates a measurable variance pattern. Fraudulent carriers show none of it. Their MC history is artificially clean: recently issued, zero growth variance, suspiciously high contact responsiveness. The Axiom Ghost Filter scores that absence as the primary fraud signal.

Ghost Score = (age_score × 0.30) + (volume_variance × 0.40) + (contact_anomaly × 0.30)
age_score = min(carrier_age_yrs × 20, 100) · volume_variance = normalized load history variance (0–100)
contact_anomaly = (10 − contact_quality) × 10 · Flag threshold: score < 35 → Ghost pattern confirmed
Key indicator: A carrier answering every call on the first ring with perfect availability across all lanes is a red flag, not a green one. Legitimate owner-operators have limited capacity windows. Unlimited availability = synthetic operation.

The Blind Spot Metric — why surface compliance masks real risk
High Asset Density (Even stages: 2, 4, 6, 8)
Carrier has trucks on the road, active authority, strong HOS availability. Physical capacity is present. Signal Integrity pressure builds underneath — driver is overextended, comms are going through the motions.
Low Asset Density (Odd stages: 1, 3, 5, 7)
Physical capacity is constrained or in flux — scaling, recovering, or restructuring. Signal Integrity is stronger. Driver or dispatcher is engaged, honest about limitations, communicating proactively.
Stage 9 — Exit / Reset: Both conditions present simultaneously. Pressure neutralized. Carrier is restructuring or exiting.
This is the only stage where Asset Density and Signal Integrity are both acknowledged as limited. No hidden tension. The carrier is transparent about their exit or transition. Plan your replacement now.

SAP Glossary — Freight & Logistics Edition
Industry translation of all Axiom Yield Broker terminology
Core scoring terms
Axiom Yield Score (AYS)
A composite 0–100 risk score calculated from four carrier operating metrics: HOS availability, route volatility, Signal Integrity, and recovery debt. Higher scores indicate safer dispatch windows. Scores below 40 trigger Stage 8 or Stage 9 flags — do not dispatch.
Asset Density (P.Stable)
The physical capacity of a carrier at the current moment — trucks available, HOS banked, authority active, equipment on the road. High asset density means a carrier has the physical infrastructure to execute the load. Also referred to as "trailer count," "fleet density," or "capacity depth" in standard freight terminology.
Signal Integrity (C.Stable)
The honesty, clarity, and reliability of communication from the driver or dispatcher. High Signal Integrity means check calls are accurate, ETAs are realistic, and problems are disclosed proactively. Low Signal Integrity — drivers going silent, dispatchers overpromising — is a primary failure predictor regardless of surface capacity metrics.
Blind Spot Metric (Inversion Principle)
The core risk mechanism behind Stage 8 detection. When Asset Density is high (carrier looks great on paper), Signal Integrity pressure accumulates invisibly underneath. The carrier's surface compliance score is inversely correlated with their true failure risk at Stage 8. This is the metric load boards cannot see — and the reason AYS was built.
Recovery Debt
Accumulated operational fatigue measured in days since the carrier's last recovery run — a light, short-haul load with home time or extended rest. High recovery debt is the primary driver of Stage 8 failure events: breakdowns, service failures, and sudden carrier dropout.
Entropy Load
The aggregate pressure applied to a carrier by route difficulty: weather severity, traffic density, dock delays, and urban congestion. High entropy load accelerates stage progression — moving carriers toward Stage 8 faster than recovery debt alone.
Ghost Score
A fraud detection composite score derived from MC age, load volume variance, and contact anomaly index. Scores below 35 indicate a synthetic carrier operation — no authentic growth history, perfect availability, and a recently issued authority number.
Lane Pressure Index
A corridor-level risk score tracking demand volume against available capacity on a given freight lane. Used to set rate multipliers 24–48 hours ahead of market-visible capacity shortages. Stage 8 lanes warrant 1.15x–1.25x base rate.
Risk stage quick-reference
Stage 0 — Ghost / No Authority
No verifiable operational history. MC number not traceable to authentic freight activity. Do not use under any circumstances.
Stage 1 — New Entrant
Recently issued authority. Clear intent, limited track record. Pilot loads only — short haul, forgiving windows, high-touch dispatch.
Stage 2 — Established / Building
Active authority, growing load history. Reliable for standard volume lanes. Not yet ready for time-critical or high-value freight.
Stage 3 — Scaling / Volatile Growth
Carrier expanding rapidly — adding trucks, routes, drivers. Over-extension risk is elevated. Monitor load volume against stated capacity closely.
Stage 4 — Prime Carrier
Peak operational reliability. Strong HOS, high Signal Integrity, low recovery debt. Assign your highest-value, tightest-window loads here. This is the dispatch sweet spot.
Stage 5 — Threshold / Point of No Return
Carrier is at the operational breakpoint. Recovery is still possible — a light load with home time can pull them back to Stage 4. Miss this window and the carrier progresses to Stage 6 or 7. Do not assign hard-deadline freight. This is the last off-ramp before irreversible degradation.
Stage 6 — Peak Performance Window
Brief window of maximum output following a recovery cycle. Carrier is executing at their highest capability. Assign your most complex or highest-value load now — this window closes within hours to days. Rotate the next carrier into position immediately.
Stage 7 — Solo Operator / Peak Isolation
Carrier is withdrawing from regular communication cycles. Demonstrated mastery of their routes and equipment — execution quality is high when engaged, but check-call responsiveness is declining. Assign solo, long-haul runs with minimal dispatch touchpoints. Individuation phase: this carrier operates best with autonomy. Watch for fragmentation if over-managed.
Stage 8 — Eye of the Storm / Do Not Dispatch
The highest-risk stage in the framework. Surface metrics are clean — HOS looks fine, paperwork is current, the carrier answers calls. This is the illusion of permanence. Peak rigidity has set in: the carrier is no longer adapting to route conditions, fatigue signals, or load demands. A service failure, breakdown, or sudden dropout is imminent. The Blind Spot Metric is maxed. Do not assign regardless of load board history.
Stage 9 — Exit / Dissolution
Carrier is deactivating, restructuring, or in process of authority surrender. May also represent a forced reset following a Stage 8 failure event. Begin replacement carrier sourcing immediately. Rare exception: Stage 9 can signal a breakthrough reset — a carrier who has fully acknowledged their operational limits and is rebuilding with clarity.
About Axiom Yield Broker
Predictive carrier risk intelligence for freight brokers, 3PLs, and dispatch operations
What this is

Axiom Yield Broker is a carrier risk scoring platform built on the Stanfield Axiom of Perpetuity (SAP) — a proprietary 10-stage operational risk framework applied to freight logistics. Standard load board tools look at historical compliance data. AYB looks at where the carrier is going by scoring current operating conditions against a predictive risk model.

The platform identifies Stage 8 carriers — those who appear fully compliant but are 24–48 hours from a service failure — before the load is tendered. It also flags ghost carrier patterns, tracks lane-level capacity pressure ahead of market visibility, and provides dispatch-ready guidance for every risk stage.

⊛ Axiom Yield Score

0–100 composite risk score from four dispatch metrics. Maps directly to SAP risk stage. Any score below 40 is a do-not-dispatch signal. Replaces gut-feel carrier selection with a repeatable, data-driven protocol.

◎ Ghost Carrier Filter

MC number screening against synthetic carrier patterns. Fraudulent operators show no authentic operational variance — perfect availability, zero growth history, newly issued authority. The filter scores the absence of legitimate freight activity as the primary flag.

⊞ Lane Pressure Intelligence
